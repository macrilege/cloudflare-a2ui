THE IDEA

A2UI gives the conversation something you can use.

A2UI means Agent-to-User Interface. Instead of stopping at a paragraph about a car, the assistant can present paint swatches, wheel pictures, selectors and actions right inside the conversation.

The useful part is the connection: what you say changes the controls, and what you select becomes the car the AI sees in your next message.

  1. You describe the car.

    “Build an orange Mustang GT V8 fastback with automatic transmission and Sinister Bronze wheels.” Typed messages and suggestion buttons both call live AI.

  2. AI interprets your request.

    It returns structured choices, such as engine: V8 and color: orange. It can also recognize a request to see inventory. It does not invent Ford configuration tokens.

  3. The app turns those choices into A2UI.

    The server validates the choices against this demo’s supported options, then sends data and a description of the controls. Our renderer displays them using the Ford-inspired styling you see in the chat.

  4. You keep refining the same car.

    Say “make it manual” and AI changes the transmission while keeping your paint and wheels. Or select a control directly: it updates the same vehicle immediately, without another AI call.

  5. Your build continues on Ford.com.

    Once the choices match a captured configuration, the app attaches its exact Build & Price URL. The configuration token travels in that link. Ford handles current pricing and the next shopping steps.

What is live, and what is prepared?

Live AI
Every submitted message and suggestion is interpreted when you send it. Follow-ups include your current vehicle choices. Replies describe validated changes.
Prepared controls and data
Developers define the allowed components, appearance and vehicle options. AI chooses intent and preferences; the app builds the A2UI layout. This demo does not ask AI to write HTML or design arbitrary screens.
Real configuration links
Hundreds of Mustang and Explorer configurations were captured from Ford’s configurator. The app selects an exact matching URL. It cannot create every possible Ford build or decode arbitrary tokens.
One inventory snapshot
The Mustang dealer card is a captured listing, not a live stock search and not a guaranteed match to your build. Equipment buttons are interactive; availability still needs checking at the dealer.

What happens when choices conflict?

Try asking for “an EcoBoost Mustang with a manual transmission.” The card explains that EcoBoost requires automatic and withholds the build link until you resolve it. Your other preferences stay in place.

Explorer’s 20-inch wheels require 4WD and the Active Comfort Package. Choosing them updates the drive and shows a notice. Switching to rear-wheel drive restores standard wheels. Explicitly asking for both RWD and 20-inch wheels produces a clarification.

The demo shows nine Mustang paints and five Explorer Active paints. Wheel options depend on the model and body style. Dimmed wheel cards have no verified configuration link for those choices yet; they are not claims that Ford cannot build them.

Why use A2UI here?

It separates what the assistant wants to show from how the interface looks. A2UI carries component descriptions and data; the application renders trusted, accessible controls. The same conversation can move between a Mustang configurator, Explorer choices and an inventory card.

Under the hood, in plain English

This demo implements a subset of A2UI v0.9. A ChoicePicker becomes a native selector or visual radio cards. Its selection is bound to a value in the vehicle data.

updateDataModel carries the latest choices; updateComponents describes the controls. Updates reuse the same surface ID, so a follow-up continues the existing interface. Local selection changes and button actions update that surface too.

The AI service is called through our server. Its output is validated before rendering. Model-supplied HTML, scripts and shopping URLs are not accepted. AI calls share a 30-per-day demo allowance; changing card controls uses no model calls.

What would a production integration add?

A current vehicle catalog, an authorized configuration service to obtain tokens, and a live inventory API. Those services would provide validated results for the same chat controls. This demo demonstrates the interaction without purchasing a vehicle, reserving stock or contacting a dealer.
